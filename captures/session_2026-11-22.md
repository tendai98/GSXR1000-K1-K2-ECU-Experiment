# Bench session log – 22 November 2026

Chronological record of every test and the frame it produced. Many of
these captures were read live and not written to a `.bin` file, so this
table is the only record of them. Times are local, from the capture
output; "~" means approximate.

ground on pin 35. Pin 56 → 1 kΩ → Uno pin 1 (Uno RESET → GND),
`/dev/ttyACM0`, 7812 baud, listen-only.

## Pin 28 (starter signal), pin 59 correction, and the burnt resistors

- Early "0 V" readings on pin 28 were on pin 27 (ground) next door.
- Real pin 28: 2.439 V open, 1.731 V with 9.87 kΩ to pin 35; 10.21 V
  when fed from 12 V through 9.87 kΩ (about 0.2 mA in).
- 12 V through 9.87 kΩ held on pin 28 for 5 s with c23 set: no change.
  The manual needs 4 s of cranking without a signal for c11 and c12.
- Pin 59: a true 1 kΩ to pin 54 gives 0 V and does not clear c23. A
  divider (pin 48 – 5.04 kΩ – pin 59 – 1 kΩ – pin 54) gives 0.8 V and
  byte 4 went `FC` → `7C` after a power cycle.
- Before the long starter hold could be run, the 10 Ω supply resistors
  began burning up. The K1's 5 V supply had shorted (see the end of this
  log). Everything measured after this point until 23 Nov came from a
  K3 ECU connected by mistake and has been moved to
  `../K3-K4/captures/session_2026-11-22_K3.md`.

## Case and processor research (23 Nov)

- Case: solid plastic shell, rubbery gel in front of the connectors.
  Not opened.
- Web search found nothing on Denso 112100-0820 or 32920-40F internals,
  and no flash kit for 2001–02. 2005–06 threads say the factory pins
  can write the flash but not read it. Details in README section 1.
- Later on 23 Nov, mr_hollywood's site and Drive folder turned up
  (saved in `reference/mr_hollywood/`, summary in README 6.17). His
  documents correct the line above: on this family reading does not
  erase anything. Points that matter for this bench:
  - Processor is MC68HC916R3 (flash) or MC68HC16R3 (mask ROM); the
    label does not say which. His dumped list includes 40F30-0820.
  - Pin 29 is the programming voltage, only used for erase/flash;
    0.5 V over destroys the ECU. Leave it unconnected.
  - Pin 30 is a pulled-up input, grounded at power-up to select the
    loader. Our earlier ~35 Ω reading disagrees; meter it first.
  - Pin 31 is the loader's K-line.
  - 12 V on pin 21 (map select) destroys the ECU. Pin 21 is near
    pin 28, where 12 V was used. A possible cause of the K1 failure,
    not proven.

## Things that went wrong, for next time

- "X to pin 54" was read as a single resistor to ground. Draw the
  whole divider path and state the voltage expected at the junction.
- A probe one pin off (27 for 28) gave several false readings. Check a
  neighbouring ground pin reads 0 V to confirm the count.
- A weak source collapses under a 10 kΩ load. Load-test a pin before
  designing a divider for it.
- Check which ECU is on the bench at the start of every session. A K3
  was measured as if it were the K1 for most of a night.
- ¼ W 10 Ω supply resistors burn at the ECU's normal 190 mA.
- A capture fails with "device reports readiness to read but returned no
  data" when another script or serial monitor has the port open.
- A resistor recorded as 1 kΩ was probably 10.2 kΩ, and "51Ko" was
  5.04 kΩ, not 510 Ω. Meter every resistor and write the reading down.
- A short power cycle may not reset the ECU. Leave it off ten seconds.
- Opening any sensor input, even for a moment while swapping parts,
  latches its fault until the next power cycle.

## K1 failure (23 Nov)

- From the burnt resistors onward, the unit on the bench was a K3 ECU
  hooked up by mistake. Those notes were moved to
  `../K3-K4/captures/session_2026-11-22_K3.md`; nothing measured on the
  K3 remains in this log.
- K1, unpowered: pin 17 to pin 35 6.36 MΩ, OL reversed.
- K1 powered through the 12 V 5 W bulb, nothing else connected: bulb
  dim, pin 17 6.51 V, pin 48 1.813 V.
- K1, unpowered: pin 48 to pin 35 2.3 Ω, 2.5 Ω reversed. The 5 V sensor
  supply is shorted inside the ECU. That is what burned the ¼ W supply
  resistors. Case cannot be opened, so the K1 is retired.
- Decision: firmware/map reading over pin 31 is not being pursued; the
  project continues as diagnostics only, on the K3.
